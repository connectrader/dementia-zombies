Infinite top-down survival · v3.0.0

Dementia Zombies

Every kill makes you stronger, slowly. Every second outside makes you forget. The world out there keeps moving: day and night, weather, survivors, hordes, and things with names. Your dementia climbs the longer you stay out, which makes the dead hungrier, the loot better, and every kill worth more experience. The house is always somewhere close. You just won't remember where.

How this works

The world keeps moving while you're outside: day and night, rain, fog and storms, survivors who fight beside you and can turn, spitters, screamers, bloaters, stalkers, tanks, elites and named hunters. Hard stretches (Horde Surges, Blood Moons, hunts) alternate with easy ones (Calm, Supply Drops, Good Memories that make you unstoppable for 25 seconds). Level comes from kills and only goes up, and it takes a while. Damage, health and cash are tracked on a log scale, so they grow forever without ever hitting infinity. Each dementia stage makes every kill worth +50% more experience. Dementia only goes down with meds. Everything else scales forever.

Gunfire is loud. Every zombie within earshot locks onto you and gets faster for a while. Quiet guns (nail gun, carbine, anything Hushed) and the knife keep the street calm. The more demented you get, the more the zombies look like ordinary people.

Weapons are forged from 97 frames and their variants (over a thousand weapon types, from frying pans and katanas that throw red waves to railguns and blackhole pistols), 36 materials, 16 conditions from Broken to Absolute, 16 elements and up to eight of roughly 385 effects, each with its own look. Early finds are plain; strong effects and rare conditions only show up as your level rises. You also wear gear in ten slots (head, body, hands, legs, feet, amulet, two rings, a trinket and a backpack) across 12 tiers: ten normal ones, then Heirloom pieces that grow with you and Oblivion pieces with a power and a price. Press I for your bag; sell at any house counter. Every level gives one skill point for the four trees (press K). Each tree ends in a capstone with no rank cap. Saves live in six slots, and any run can be exported as a save file or a save code.

Weapons bond with you. Every kill with a weapon raises its bond, and a bonded weapon keeps catching up to your level on its own, so the one you like stays good. Bond milestones give a perk you pick at the workbench (30 perks, from Honed to Part of You), and at bond 10 it earns a nickname. The workbench also tunes, sharpens, re-forges and infuses weapons, upgrades gear, and salvages junk for parts.

The street has a rhythm. Quiet, then Stirring, then Roaming, where fewer but tougher zombies hunt you, and sometimes a Swarm of weak ones you can mow down. Mowing gets tiring: kill too many too fast and experience drops for a bit. Coming home safe pays a made-it-home bonus.

The house is yours, wherever it wanders. Press E inside for 11 rooms: counter, workbench, weapon wall, bedroom (sleep for Well Rested), roommates (survivors you bring home, with 16 jobs), kennel (10 kinds of pet), radio bounties, the memory wall (8 chapters of your story, each a permanent bonus), trophy shelf, garage lab and renovations.

Bosses, crafting, minimap and sound. Every named one has its own trick: Big Earl charges down a red line, the Mailman lobs parcels into circles, the Night Nurse heals the dead around her, Pastor Gene's sermons halve your damage, the Landlord takes rent out of your cash with every hit, the Tall Man steps out right behind you, and Dr. Whitlock's syringes make you forget faster. The Crafting tab at home turns materials into weapons, armor, relics (or exactly the relic you want), quirks, Heirlooms, Oblivion gear, meds, unmeds, throwables and ammo, and renders junk back into materials. M toggles the minimap. N toggles sound, and the pause menu sets the volume.

Growth and loot. When you level up or your dementia jumps, the dead don't jump with you. They catch up over a minute or so, while your loot improves at once, so fast growth is a window of strength. The slip shows how far behind they are. The bag screen can also auto-recycle or auto-sell loot below a quality you pick as you walk over it, and recycle everything below a cutoff in one click anywhere. Heirlooms sell and recycle like anything else (sweeps leave them alone unless you tick "Heirlooms count as junk"), and recycling one gives heirloom parts for eight heirloom recipes in the Crafting tab: family heirlooms, bonded heirloom weapons, Mythic relics of your choice, deeper Mementos, family traits and more. Every weapon and piece of gear has a peak: it stops growing at a set number of levels above where it dropped, more for better condition, so sooner or later you trade up. Going down gets riskier each time on a trip, and the fourth time is for real. Every zombie has a chance to drop materials (cloth, bone, teeth, batteries, bile, elite essence, memory dust, void shards) as well as weapons and gear. The bag screen lists what you carry and the exact odds per zombie. Some merchants want materials instead of, or as well as, cash: the Bone Witch, the Scrap King, the Seamstress, the Rag-and-Bone Man, the Relic Peddler and The Stranger.

Relics, quirks and merchants. Two relic slots hold strange keepsakes, 32 of them, from Ellen's Locket (once a trip, a killing blow stops time) to the Jar of Teeth (kills raise zombies on your side) and the Cowbell. Better armor and accessories can roll quirks, 37 of them: Phasing, Frostskin, Corpse-bomb, Pyrewalking, Night-owl, Gambling and more. The counter sells relics and six pieces of gear. Out in the city, wandering merchants set up small safe camps: nothing can hurt you inside the circle, and you heal by the fire. Eleven kinds, from the Traveling Tinker to the Fortune Teller's mystery boxes, an ice cream truck, a hermit who only takes parts, and something called The Stranger.

Unmeds. The opposite of your pills: fog pills, haze tonics, black syrup, Lethe draughts, brainstorms and Oblivion ampoules push your dementia up or make it build faster. Meaner dead, better loot, faster levels. U takes one, J switches. The counter's stock, roommates' finds, places, bounties and crates all scale with your dementia now, the same way drops do.

Classes. Pick a main class in the Classes tab at home: 12 to start (Survivor, Gunslinger, Brawler, Medic, Sniper, Demolitionist, Engineer, Bulwark, Scout, Pyromancer, Occultist, Scavenger). Your main class levels with every bit of experience you earn and keeps its level when you switch. Passives grow every level up to 100, where the class is mastered; its skill on F ranks up every 10 levels, and level 50 unlocks a signature. Take two classes to 25 and an advanced class opens (12 of them, like Gun-Kata, Reaper and Plague Doctor). At level 25 you can also set a secondary class for 35% of its passives. Every class you take to 50 makes all the others stronger.

Playing together. Everyone's ping shows next to their name. If you die in a co-op world you are not out: you drop a tenth of your cash and wake up in the house eight seconds later. Players below the highest level in the world get catch-up help that grows with the gap: more experience, more damage, less damage taken and better loot. Z drops a marker everyone can see, Y opens chat. The host can kick players from the Co-op screen, and a player whose connection drops gets eight seconds to come back before their spot is given up.

Dimensions. From level 20, rifts tear open out in the world. Step in (E) and you are somewhere else for a few minutes: the Goblin Market, the Deep Woods, the Boneyard, the Grey Zone, Nightfall Manor or the Pit, each with its own dead and its own rules, much better loot and more experience, and no house to run to. The way back opens after about a minute and a half. Stay too long and the place throws you out.

Every dimension is its own place. Each one looks different and is the only place certain effects drop. All lifesteal (Vampiric weapons, the Leech on gear and the Blood element) comes from Nightfall Manor. Hellfire and Explosive come from the Pit, Seeking, Phantom and Gravitic from the Grey Zone, Unbroken and Executing from the Boneyard, Fleet and Deadshot from the Deep Woods, Lucky and Scavenging from the Goblin Market. Things you already own keep what they have.

Dungeons. From level 6, a way down opens nearby every few minutes: a root cellar, the storm drains, a hospital basement or a fallout shelter. Each is a fixed set of rooms with a fixed number of the dead, and nothing else comes. A named horror waits in the last room. Clear every one and a chest opens with a pile of loot, cash, parts and experience. Climb back out by the ladder any time. Deeper dungeons (every 40 levels) have more rooms and better chests. From level 8 some ways down are stealth runs: a heist, a ghost run or an assassination.

Settings. Pause → Settings has sound and music volume, screen shake, damage numbers, flashes, reduce motion, camera lean, a compact HUD and a frame-rate counter. Every keyboard key can be moved there too, and the controller has its own screen.

Surviving. The dead wind up before they bite: they rear back and a red wedge shows where the lunge will land. Step out of it, roll through it with Space, or shove them back with V or right click, which staggers them and cancels the bite. Rolling and shoving cost stamina (the arc under you). Guns have magazines: R reloads, and pressing it again while the marker is in the bright band is a perfect reload, which is instant and makes the next magazine hit 15% harder. Miss the band and you fumble.

Night. In daylight the dead thin out. At night they come out in force, and it is dark: you see what your flashlight sees, and their eyes in the dark. L turns the flashlight off, which makes you harder to spot. Day kills give a little more experience, night gives numbers and better loot. Darkness and camera distance can be changed in Settings.

Ruth. There's a story now: eight chapters about finding your wife, running alongside the endless game. It starts the first time you step outside. The Journal (Pause or the quick menu) keeps track of where you are.

Cars. Some parked cars still run. Walk up and press E to try one; a dead one can be siphoned for gas. Push the way you want to go and the car turns and drives there; push back to brake. Running the dead over hurts them and the car. Cars burn fuel, take damage from everything, and make a lot of noise. E gets out.

Sieges. Every third night, and every blood moon, they come for the house if you're near it. Hold the door until the last wave is down for supply crates and cash. Let it break and they take some of your cash. The Fortify tab at home builds a heavier barricade, a spike ring, corner turrets and floodlights, and the dinner bell there starts a siege whenever you like.

Raids. The Raids tab at home sends you into an arena with one of three bosses: The Matron, Sawbones and The Choir, opening at levels 15, 30 and 50. Each has three phases. Red circles slam, red lines are charges, and spit comes in rings and spirals you roll through. Each one drops a signature weapon you can't get anywhere else. Raids are single player for now.

The street. Red barrels blow up when shot and set off any barrel near them; propane tanks leave the ground burning. Stand clear, they hurt you too. Parked cars can have alarms: hit one and it might start screaming, and every one of the dead nearby goes to it while more arrive. Next to you that's bad news. Down the street it's a lure. A red arc around you shows where a hit came from.

Hordes. Every minute or so a pack comes at you from one direction, and you get a warning saying which way. The stronger you get, the more the dead answer with numbers rather than toughness: bigger crowds, faster spawns, bigger packs, more elites and more traits stacked on them. Single zombies keep getting easier; the answer to the crowd is effects. Piercing, Twin, Arcing, Splitting, Ricocheting and Explosive all get stronger on weapons in better condition.

Soft caps. Bonuses from gear, counter treatments and the endless skills count in full up to a point, then each extra bit is worth less. A weapon or piece of gear you keep tuning, or that keeps up with you through its bond, gains less from every level past where it dropped, so new finds catch up with old favourites.

Loot goblins. They show up in every dimension and now and then at home. They never fight back. They run, and after about 20 seconds they open a portal and escape. Catch one and it bursts with loot: about ten items, or a fortune in cash, weapons, armor, relics or materials depending on the kind. Hitting one spills a little as you chase it.

Crazed survivors. From level 4, armed survivors who lost it come after you with a real weapon for your level. They aim badly, so keep moving. They often drop the weapon they were carrying.

More of the dead. Leapers pounce, hoarders run off with full pockets (catch them), clingers slow you, riot zombies hide behind shields, burrowers travel underground and pop up beside you, hazmats leave toxic puddles, brood mothers spit out crawlers, pyres burn and explode, nurses heal the dead around them (kill them first) and phantoms flicker out of your shots. New elite traits: shielded, explosive, frostbitten, thorned, blinking, summoning and toxic. The game tells you what each one does the first time you meet it.

Streaks. Kills less than 3 seconds apart chain into a streak. Each kill in a streak gives 1% more experience, up to 60%, and big streaks get called out. Early levels come faster while you find your feet.

Controller. Any controller works: Xbox, PlayStation, Switch Pro, 8BitDo and generic USB pads. Left stick moves, right stick aims, the right trigger fires (turn on "Right stick fires" under Pause → Controller for twin-stick style), the left trigger shoves. Bottom face button uses, right face button rolls, left face button reloads, top face button swaps weapons, left bumper throws, right bumper fires your class skill, clicking the left stick sneaks, clicking the right stick picks a throwable. D-pad left and right pick a weapon, up takes meds, down uses a medkit. Unmeds, skills and the flashlight are in the quick menu. Start always opens the pause screen, from play or from any menu. Select (View, Share, Minus) opens the quick menu: every screen, plus actions for wherever you're standing, so nothing needs a button of its own. In menus the D-pad or left stick moves, the bottom button presses, the right button goes back, the bumpers switch tabs at the house and the right stick scrolls. On-screen prompts use your controller's own button names. Pause → Controller shows a live input test, rebinds any button (layouts are saved per controller), and sets aim assist, stick dead zone and vibration. Music volume is in the pause menu.

Party and co-op. Take up to 3 roommates along from the Party tab at home. They follow you, fight with the weapon you hand them, and each class has a trick: medics heal, tanks taunt, grenadiers lob frags, callers bring airstrikes. If you drop, you go down instead of dying while anyone in the party is still standing; they have 30 seconds to reach you. Press C for co-op: host a game or join one, up to 4 players, and friends take over roommate spots. It runs through claude.ai's live room, so friends need this page shared with them.

Places and throwables. 22 kinds of place dot the city, each guarded, each with a themed cache that restocks every 4 days. 16 throwables, from molotovs and noisemakers to bear traps, airstrikes and a suitcase nuke. G throws, T switches.
